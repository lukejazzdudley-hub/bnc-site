Repeat a vowel or repeat a consonant

Assonance is repetition of vowel sounds. In “slow boats roll home,” the stressed OH sound connects the words while the endings change. Consonance repeats consonant sounds, as in “a quick click in the lock,” where K keeps coming back. If the stressed vowel and the rest of the ending match closely, the same passage may also contain a perfect or near rhyme.

Read the same idea three ways

  1. Plain: “I took the road back to the house.”
  2. Vowel-led: “I drove home slow through the smoke.”
  3. Consonant-led: “I cut back quick before the clock struck.”

Record all three against the same loop. In the vowel-led line, try stretching “home.” In the consonant-led line, let the K sounds fall on the beat, then move one between beats. If the words start to tangle, remove one repeated consonant and compare. These are options for directing attention. You can use both patterns or leave a line plain so a later repeat stands out.

Try sound repetition without repeating the ending

“Slow boats roll home / I watched them from the pier” keeps the OH sound in the first line and leaves the second ending open. “A quick click in the lock / then the whole room went still” uses a cluster of K sounds before the pause. Leave the line endings alone and move one repeated sound closer to the action. Listen for whether the change directs attention to the image or merely makes the line busier.

Check the recording in your own accent

A written match can disappear at performance speed. Mark the stressed syllables, record twice and listen with the lyric hidden. Try the English rhyme finder for candidates or follow the lesson on syllables, stress and flow to revise the delivery.
