Start with your lyric and take

Open a song and bring in its lyric, beat and vocal ideas. If the starting point is a voice memo, transcribe it and correct the words against the recording. Follow the import and transcription guide.

The clips below show the arrangement and DAW stages of Springtime Showers. They do not document its original transcription.

Shape the arrangement, then open the DAW

Use Arrange to organise the song sections. Listen through their order before moving into the DAW, where the beat and vocal takes become clips you can work with on a timeline.

If a DAW session already exists, read the replacement confirmation before rebuilding from Arrange. Preserve manual edits and exported versions you want to keep.

Listen to the vocal in context

Open the channel and effects controls. Compare the processed sound with the dry vocal, then return to the whole mix. Changes that sound impressive in isolation may make a lyric less clear when the beat returns.

Watch sections become aligned WAV stems

Follow the steps

  1. Open Arrange. Minimise the beat player if it covers the song tools. Choose Arrange into a song draft.
  2. Check assigned takes. This existing draft uses the lead vocal for Verse and the hook vocal for Hook. The recording begins with those assignments already saved; it does not show their original recording.
  3. Build in DAW. The section lanes appear alongside the existing beat and vocal lanes. This session retains those original lanes: review which versions should play before making a combined mix, so the same take is not doubled unintentionally.
  4. Export stems. Choose Export audio, mix or stems, then Export stems (WAV · aligned at 0). Wait for Stems exported.
  5. Find the files. Open Files → Cadence → Exports. Import all the WAV files at time zero in your other DAW. Keep their leading silence; choose or mute duplicate versions deliberately.

Fresh files from this final arrangement were checked on 17 October: all six are 48 kHz, 32-bit floating-point WAV, 206.797 seconds long and contain non-silent audio. The beat is stereo; the five vocal/section stems are mono. Matching lengths allow a common time-zero import. File-format and level checks do not certify a finished performance or clipping-free mix.

Try the same workflow with one of your ideas

Choose one recording you want to finish. Put it with its lyric, decide where the hook belongs, and make a rough demo you can listen to away from the editor. A useful next version can tell you more than another week of isolated notes.

What does the Springtime Showers walkthrough show?

The real Cadence captures show a song moving from arrangement into a multitrack DAW and its vocal-effects controls. They do not document the original transcription or prove an audio-quality comparison.

Should I preserve an existing DAW session before rebuilding?

Yes. Read the replacement confirmation before rebuilding from Arrange. Keep exports and any manual edits you want to preserve.
