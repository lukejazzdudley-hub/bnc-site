Lyrics, rhyme choices and beat loops together

A lyric notepad is useful until the idea needs a beat, a second take or an ending you have not found yet. Cadence keeps those steps with the song. Write the verse, explore pronunciation-based rhyme families and return to the same lyric while the instrumental plays.

Perfect rhymes, slant rhymes, multisyllabic matches, multi-word rhymes and assonance provide different options. A suggestion still needs to work in your accent and sentence. Read it aloud before keeping it.

How to start a song on Android

  1. Open the Library, choose New and give your lyric a title.
  2. Open the song’s quick actions and choose Beat → Load beat to import an instrumental you can use.
  3. Set the A–B loop around the passage you want to write. Use Rhymes when you need another sound, then record a rough take.
  4. When the draft is ready, arrange the sections and continue into the DAW. Draft Demo and DAW creation require Pro.

See beat import and looping, the rhyme inspector and the recorded arrangement and export walkthrough. Menu placement can differ by installed version; the guide names the controls to look for.

Write and record over your beat

Import a beat audio file, use the waveform to find the part you need and set independent A–B loop points. Draft while listening, then record a take over the instrumental. Comparing the recording with the words helps you hear where timing or breath needs attention.

Continue into Draft Demo, arrangement and the mobile DAW when you want to build the next version. See the type-beat writing workflow for an exercise you can try with your own instrumental.

Looking for a GarageBand alternative on Android?

Start with the part of GarageBand you need to replace. For writing lyrics, keeping ideas together and testing a vocal over a beat, Cadence provides a connected writing workflow. For instrument libraries, instrument performance or a specific production plugin, check the required feature directly rather than assuming that every music app is interchangeable.

Cadence has a mobile multitrack workspace; this does not make it a feature-for-feature GarageBand clone. The Cadence and GarageBand comparison explains the different jobs. The BandLab comparison is useful if you are choosing between writing and a broader production workflow.

Offline writing and your recordings

The store listing describes offline writing, an on-device rhyme engine and downloadable language packs. Download the packs you need while connected. Account access, purchases and downloads need a connection; offline writing does not mean every connected service works without one.

Recordings remain on your device unless you explicitly share or upload them. Signing in elsewhere is not a recording backup. Export important work before changing devices. For recorded ideas, follow the audio import and transcription workflow; Android share options depend on the app you export from.
